Does a low April 1 mean a lower spring peak?

Lake Muskoka at Bala, 2004–2025: the April 1 level against the spring peak.

No. Whether the lake starts April low or high, the spring peak lands in about the same place: typically +6.3 in against the July average. What the April 1 level does predict is how far the lake rises to get there.

There is no April 1, 2026 reading at Bala yet. Environment Canada publishes each year's daily means about seven months after the year ends, and the realtime feed keeps only a month, so 2026's spring will appear here when 2026 is published.

How this is worked out

For each year, the July average is the mean of every July day in the 5 years before it, the same benchmark the daily email uses, so it is what was known on April 1. The peak is the highest daily mean at Bala from April 2 to May 31. The snowmelt crest falls in that window in nearly every year.

start = level(Apr 1) − July avg peak = max(level, Apr 2 … May 31) − July avg rise = peak − start

The link is a correlation: 1 means the two always move together, −1 means one always goes up as the other goes down, 0 means no pattern. The tiles and trend lines use only the 17 years with a full 5-year July benchmark. Hollow dots had fewer Julys to average, because the record starts in 2003 and some Julys are incomplete.

Why it works this way: Lake Muskoka is regulated by the dams at Bala and Port Carling. The level is drawn down over winter to make room for the melt and refilled in spring, so the April 1 level mostly reflects how far it was drawn down. How high the peak goes depends more on snowpack and spring rain, as the flood of 2019 shows.

22 years is a small sample, so read the numbers as a pattern, not a forecast.
